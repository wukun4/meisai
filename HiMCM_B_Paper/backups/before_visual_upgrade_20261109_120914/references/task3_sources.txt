Bibitem key: las-vegas-climate
Author/publisher: NOAA National Weather Service, Las Vegas office.
Title: Las Vegas General Climatic Summary / Climate of Las Vegas.
URL: https://www.weather.gov/vef/ClimateofLasVegas
Locators: paragraphs on the desert valley, summer heat, moisture barriers, and

Verified facts: Las Vegas has a desert setting, abundant sunshine, very hot

The official climate station is at Harry Reid International Airport, not below
the Fremont canopy. These facts motivate a hot-dry scenario, while acknowledging
that dry conditions are not universal throughout the year.
Not established: the scenario's exact dry-bulb temperature, relative humidity,

not observations from this source or from Fremont Street.

6. HONOLULU CLIMATE AND WEAK-TRADE-WIND CASE
Bibitem key: honolulu-climate
Author/publisher: NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI).
Title: Local Climatological Data: Annual Summary with Comparative Data,
Honolulu, Hawaii (PHNL), 2023.
URL: https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/access/cebrequests/2023lcdannual/01202313HNL.pdf
Locator: printed/PDF page 7, local climate narrative (zero-based PDF page 6).
Verified facts: Honolulu and Waikiki lie on Oahu's southern coastal plain;
ocean influence moderates temperatures; northeasterly trade winds commonly
provide cooling. When trades weaken or southerly flow replaces them, muggy
conditions can develop. The document concerns Honolulu airport and regional
climate; it is not an instrument record at the zoo's ticket queue.
Allowed use: motivate a warm, weak-wind maritime case contrasting with the
Las Vegas hot-dry case. Do not describe Honolulu as permanently stagnant or
uniformly high-humidity, or claim this weak-wind case is an annual average.
Not established: the assumed queue-level wind, air-change rate, temperature/RH
combination, or its frequency. These remain scenario inputs and sensitivity axes.

FACT / ASSUMPTION / OUTPUT CHECKLIST
- Sourced geometry: only the Fremont canopy length, 1,375 ft = 419.1 m.
- Qualitative sourced geometry: Honolulu's shaded public ticket-purchasing and
  queue space, distinguished from enclosed service spaces.
- Estimated geometry: all modeled widths, queue depth, height, usable area,
  effective volume, zoning, obstacle and opening fractions.
- Estimated operation: arrivals, occupancy, dwell time, exposure schedule,
  equipment counts/capacities/placements, airflows and local coverage.
- Estimated physics: representative weather, radiation, ACH/exchange rates,
  delivery mixing, surface/thermal parameters and initial conditions unless
  individually tied to an additional verified source.
- Simulation outputs: comfort/SET changes, useful cooling service U, electrical
  energy E, efficiency U/E, and strategy rankings. They are model predictions,
  not the venues' measured performance or existing installed systems.
- Comparisons must use a consistent service-energy definition and distinguish
  a literal enlargement from replicating a local service module. Actual venue
  identities do not validate assumed parameter values.
- Geometry, system operation, and microclimate must not be inferred from the
  presence of a canopy alone. The Honolulu case is the outdoor visitor queue,
  not the potentially conditioned employee booth.

Suggested concise wording for the paper:
"The operator reports a 1,375-ft (419.1-m) Fremont canopy. For Honolulu Zoo,
the architect documents shaded ticket-purchasing and queue space, and official
admission information confirms continuing booth sales. All other geometric and
operating inputs are declared scenario estimates. Regional climate sources
motivate a hot-dry Las Vegas case and a warm, weak-wind Honolulu case; neither
set of inputs is presented as an on-site monitoring record."
