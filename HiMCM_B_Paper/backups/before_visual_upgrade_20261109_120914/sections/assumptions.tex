\section{Assumptions and Justifications}
\begingroup\setlength{\intextsep}{4pt}
\label{sec:assumptions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt,label=\textbf{A\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Matched service boundary.} Comparisons share weather, occupants, geometry and period; kW/hr entries mean kW. \textit{Justification:} Matched workloads and complete device-energy accounting isolate efficiency.
\item \textbf{Zoned air and rapid local mixing.} Background states are uniform within zones; footprints are disjoint and speeds prescribed. Local storage is negligible. \textit{Justification:} Mixing is faster than the station's 120-s disturbance.
\item \textbf{Conserved heat and water.} Evaporation is near-adiabatic, dry-air mass fixed and exchange balanced. Shaded radiation uses mean radiant temperature; public-space condensation is excluded. \textit{Justification:} Electrical and vapor-enthalpy sources enter once.
\item \textbf{Reference human response.} Standing users have 1.2 met and 0.5 clo; SET excess uses a $26^\circ$C reference. \textit{Justification:} Signed relief compares airflow and evaporation. Dry-heat tests use $T_a=T_r=39/40^\circ$C and RH $15/20\%$. The upper applicability boundary of the standard ASHRAE SET physiological model used here is $40^\circ$C; the interior $39^\circ$C test retains the ranking \cite{pythermal}.
\item \textbf{Explicit design inputs.} Sources establish geometry and climate context; tables specify planning estimates. Costs share a currency and horizon; clearance represents visitor access. \textit{Justification:} Explicit inputs support sensitivity analysis. SET relief is distinct from heat-safety procedures.
\end{enumerate}
