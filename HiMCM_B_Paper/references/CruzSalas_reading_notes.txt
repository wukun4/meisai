English paragraph for the model-development discussion (194 words)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Cruz-Salas et al. develop a thermodynamic model of mist cooling coupled with
natural ventilation in a semi-outdoor cafeteria \cite{cruzsalas2026}. Their
distinction between evaporative outlet conditions and conditions in the recipient
space motivates a common device-to-delivery interface in our models. Dry-air and
water conservation, approximately isenthalpic evaporation, and a wet-bulb
saturation limit provide the physical basis for the evaporative-device module.
Enthalpy and humidity ratio describe the subsequent mixing with background air.
For the bus shelter, we prescribe this background and evaluate quasi-steady local
conditions. For the rail station, we retain the device, mixing, SET, and service
calculations while allowing background enthalpy, humidity ratio, and surface
temperature to evolve with ventilation, inter-zone exchange, and passenger
loads. This extension is our reduced-order formulation; it is not the paper's
EnergyPlus implementation. Their experimental validation concerns an unoccupied
cafeteria during the dry-hot season, and the study does not evaluate predicted or
perceived thermal comfort. Consequently, its reported temperature accuracy does
not validate our occupied, humid-climate station or its SET predictions. We use
the study to support the heat-and-moisture coupling and matched no-cooling
comparison, while retaining explicitly assumed parameters and separate
recipient-response modules for food compliance and human thermal service.

Specific evidence and limits for integration
-------------------------------------------
1. PDF p4, Section 3.1.1, equations (1)-(3): isenthalpic approximation, conserved
   dry-air flow, and the water-vapor/liquid-water balance. Equations (8)-(12)
   introduce misting effectiveness and impose a wet-bulb/saturation limit.
2. PDF p4, Section 3.1.2, equation (14), and p5: Model 2 reproduces the outlet
   air state of the physical mist model using an ideal evaporative cooling
   system plus air mixing for implementation in EnergyPlus. Model 2 is an
   implementation surrogate, not a claim that the real equipment is a wet pad.
3. PDF p6, Sections 3.2.1 and 3.2.4: the cafeteria was 126 m^2; measurements
   were on 15-17 February 2025 during the dry-hot season, with neither occupancy
   nor other internal heat gains. These conditions differ from crowded,
   humid-climate Canberra station. The study does not validate our station.
4. PDF p13, Section 4.8: matched simulations with mist on/off isolate the
   contribution of mist cooling, giving maximum/mean air-temperature reductions
   of 2.9/1.9 degrees C. These are outcomes of the cafeteria experiment/model,
   not assumed performance for any of our devices or climates.
5. PDF p13: average misting-line effectiveness epsilon_m is 0.18 at the entrance
   and 0.17 at the lattice wall. Its definition is equation (8), p4: the fraction
   of supplied liquid-water flow contributing to potential evaporation within
   the represented zone (before the saturation check). It is NOT the familiar
   wet-bulb temperature-drop effectiveness of a direct evaporative cooler.
   Do not replace our assumed epsilon = 0.70 with 0.18, or cite the study as
   evidence for 0.70. The study also reports cafeteria-scale temperature
   effectiveness epsilon_ce = 0.16, defined separately by equation (13), p4.
6. PDF p15-16, Section 5: perfect mixing within each thermal zone cannot resolve
   every spatial/temporal variation. Predicted and perceived occupant comfort
   were not evaluated; additional humidity may reduce the benefit of lower
   dry-bulb temperature. This supports retaining the human SET response module,
   but does not establish its validation or transfer a cafeteria calibration.
7. The physical principles support a common heat/moisture interface. Freezer
   food-temperature compliance and human SET remain distinct response modules.
   Compliance mass-hours/kWh and person-time thermal service/kWh cannot be
   directly ranked against each other without an additional stated conversion.
